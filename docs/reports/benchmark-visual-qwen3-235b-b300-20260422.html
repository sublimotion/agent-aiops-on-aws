Key Insights

TP4 Dominates Single-Stream: 4.4x Over TP2+DP4+EP

TP4 delivers 110 tok/s vs 23 tok/s single-stream, with 168ms vs 6,007ms cold-start TTFT. The 4-way expert parallel warm-up in TP2+DP4 creates a 36x TTFT penalty at low concurrency. TP4 is the clear choice for general use.

Co-Tenancy: The Real Cost Story

Running 2× independent TP4 processes (GPUs 0-3 and 4-7) on the same node eliminates 4 idle GPUs. This doubles throughput to 23,640 tok/s while halving cost to $0.19/M tokens — strictly better than TP2+DP4+EP (13,877 tok/s at $0.33/M).

Prefix Caching: 1.8-2.0x TTFT Improvement

W2 RAG workloads show consistent 1.8-2.0× TTFT improvement with prefix caching enabled. A 10K-token document drops from 105ms cold to 58ms warm. Critical for multi-turn and RAG-heavy production workloads.

TTFT p99 at SLO Edge: 1,996ms @ c=512

TTFT p99 barely passes the 2,000ms SLO target at c=512 (1,996ms). For production with strict SLO, cap at c=256 (TTFT p99 = 922ms) or use co-tenant 2×TP4 to share the load.

EP Scaling Not Yet Saturated — Potential Crossover at Higher Concurrency

TP2+DP4+EP shows steeper throughput growth than TP4 at high concurrency: +80% from c=128→256 vs +35% for TP4. At c=512, EP already leads by 17% (13,877 vs 11,820 tok/s) and the gap is widening. The co-tenancy model (2×TP4 = 23,640 tok/s theoretical) assumes linear scaling, but two independent processes competing for NVLink bandwidth and memory bus may interfere. EP’s single coordinated scheduler could win at c=1024+. Follow-up: test c=1024 and c=2048 on both configs to find the true ceilings.

43.6x Cheaper Than Sonnet API for Coding Agents

At $4.70/eng/month supporting 2,560 engineers, self-hosted Qwen3-235B is 43.6× cheaper than Claude Sonnet API ($205/eng/mo). Break-even at just 59 engineers. For a 500-engineer org: $90K/month savings vs Sonnet, $500K/month vs Opus.
