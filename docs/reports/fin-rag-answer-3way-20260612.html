VERDICT

H200 vLLM tp1×8 is the cheapest SLO-passing deployment; B200 holds the full 130-concurrent target with margin and has the most headroom above it (SLO knee ~c201). Comparing like-for-like — cheapest SLO-passing operating point, reserved-capacity pricing — H200 (8 GPUs, tp1×8) now has a measured contractual-SLO pass through c120 at $0.056/1M total tokens (e2e p50 5.2 s / p90 7.9 s, 0 errors), about ½ of B200's cheapest SLO-passing point ($0.115/1M at B200's c192 knee). The confirming c64/c96/c120 sweep (run 2026-06-12 against the live endpoint) replaces the earlier interpolated estimate and lands almost on it (~$0.061 predicted → $0.056 measured). A 2-point fit on c120 (p90 7.9 s) and c130 (p90 10.0 s) puts H200's e2e-p90 crossover at ~c128 — it holds the SLO almost to the target, missing the exact 130 point by ~2 concurrent. B200 vLLM tp2×4 holds the e2e SLO well past the 130 spec — measured to c192 ($0.115/1M, p50 6.0 s / p90 9.1 s) with the p90 crossover at ~c201; at the c130 spec point it runs $0.126/1M (p50 4.2 s / p90 7.6 s) with the most tail margin of any platform. We confirmed tp2×4 is the right B200 layout by sweeping the collective-free tp1×8 alternative (8 TP=1 replicas) at c120/c130: it fails the e2e gate (p50 ~11.5 s, TPOT p50 ~135 ms) at roughly half the aggregate throughput of tp2×4 — so tp2×4 is the measured-optimal B200 layout, and its cheapest SLO-passing cost is $0.115/1M at the c192 knee. Two H200 caveats: the win is the tp1×8 layout specifically — the live SGLang tp2×4 config is ~2.6× slower and fails by c64; and the supporting TPOT p99 (50 ms) is exceeded at these concurrencies on every platform (the same "PASS*" as B200). g7e.12xlarge (2 GPUs) runs a single TP=2 replica that saturates by ~8 concurrent, passing only at c8 ($0.158/1M); its lower "$0.105" is a saturated, SLO-failing c256 point. Choose H200 tp1×8 for the best $/token within SLO (~½ of B200, proven to c120). Choose B200 if you must hold the 130-concurrent target with margin today — and note it has ~55% more headroom above the spec (to ~c201) at an even lower $0.115/1M. g7e is a low-concurrency / data-residency play, viable for a lightly-loaded single-user chatbot but not for this load on one node.

The "half the bytes → half the time" intuition does not hold here measured

FP8 runs at 1.38× bf16 throughput at c8 and 1.24× at c130 — bf16 delivers ~72–81% of FP8, not ~50%. The 2× guess comes from FP8 weights being half the size (124 GB vs 240 GB), which converts to 2× throughput only if decode is purely weight-bandwidth-bound. This workload is prefill-dominated and launch/scheduling-bound at the knee (see the DCGM section: HBM ~15%, tensor ~11%), so halving the bytes doesn't halve the time — the bottleneck isn't bytes. The tell is the ratio compressing as load rises (1.38× → 1.24×): a bandwidth-bound gap would hold or widen; it shrinking confirms the launch-bound regime where precision matters less.

What is ~2×: VRAM, not speed. BF16 ~240 GB vs FP8 ~124 GB — a capacity fact (it's why bf16 needs the full tp2×4 footprint and can't fit a single 96 GB g7e GPU). And the SLO verdict flips at peak: bf16 fails the e2e-p90 gate at c130 (10.5 s) while FP8 passes (8.05 s). So for the 130-concurrent contract, FP8 isn't merely faster — it's the only one of the two that holds the target.

Scope caveat: bf16 was characterized on B200 at c8 and c130 only — no H200/g7e bf16, no intermediate concurrencies. The endpoints are solid; the exact bf16 SLO knee between c8 and c130 was not swept.

What moved the numbers — and what didn't

Three optimization levers were investigated for this hybrid-Mamba MoE model. Only one paid off; the other two are structurally blocked today. Being explicit about this matters: the throughput above is what well-batched dense prefill delivers, with no help from caching or speculation.

✅ Chunked-prefill batching tuning — the lever that worked production config

For ~9K-token RAG prefills, the batched-token budget is the dominant tunable. Sweeping --max-num-batched-tokens (B200, FP8 tp2×4, c130):
max-num-batched-tokense2e p50e2e p90TTFT p50TPOTcontractual SLO
4,0967,06511,93368483.0FAIL (both gates)

A small chunk budget fragments each long prefill into many chunked iterations, inflating TTFT and the p90 tail; 4,096 fails both SLO gates. Setting --max-num-batched-tokens 16384 (== max-model-len) lets a prefill complete in one pass → lowest TTFT and tightest tail. This is the promoted production config and the reason B200 holds the SLO at 130 concurrent.

⛔ Automatic prefix caching — non-functional for this Mamba-2 model, at every TP including TP=1

The workload has a shared ~3,050-token system/RAG header, so prefix caching should cut prefill cost. It doesn't here: vLLM reports 0 prefix-cache hits across all replicas, on both B200 and g7e, on vLLM 0.18.1 and 0.22.1. The flags are accepted (--enable-prefix-caching --mamba-cache-mode all, enable_prefix_caching=True in the engine config) but the hybrid Mamba-2 cache path never realizes hits. We confirmed this is not TP-specific: the recommended H200 tp1×8 (TP=1) deployment, swept 2026-06-12, scraped vllm:prefix_cache_hits_total = 0 against 4.2M prefix_cache_queries_total — zero hits at TP=1 with a verbatim shared header, so the limitation is the Mamba-2 hybrid architecture itself, not the TP>1 KV layout (a stronger statement than the earlier "TP>1 only" read; cf. upstream #26201). The cost figures in this report therefore include no prefix-cache benefit — the SLO rests on raw prefill throughput, g7e's weakest axis. (We fail-close on cache inflation: any reported hit rate above the ~30% real-corpus ceiling is treated as instrumentation error, not a win.)

⛔ Speculative decoding — not deployable on vLLM 0.18/0.22 at TP=2

Both speculative paths crash on the Nemotron-3-Super hybrid (Mamba-2 + select-attention) layout:
  • Native MTP — the FP8 checkpoint ships MTP weights (1,042 mtp.* tensors), vLLM loads the drafter, then crashes on the first forward: tensor a (2) must match tensor b (3) — a shape mismatch in the MTP draft forward at TP=2.
  • n-gram / prompt-lookup (no draft model, TP-agnostic) — crashes in CUDA-graph capture: the Mamba-2 attention backend assumes a fixed single-token decode shape and can't reconcile the multi-token speculative query (tensor a (2) must match tensor b (6)).
Acceptance was still measurable via an --enforce-eager workaround (graph capture off), but eager latency isn't production-representative. Net: spec-decode is the secondary lever for a prefill-dominated workload anyway — re-test only when a newer vLLM fixes the Mamba-2 graph-capture + spec-decode path. Confirmed upstream that 0.22.1 does not fix this, so no rebase was warranted.

g7e engine bake-off — SGLang beats vLLM, and only one MoE backend works on sm_120

SGLang v0.5.12.post1-cu130 wins on g7e key finding

SGLang is faster than vLLM at every g7e operating point (c8 e2e p50 4,607 vs 7,100 ms) — radix cache plus a working FP8 MoE kernel. NCCL initialized cleanly: the cu130 image ships NCCL ≥2.26.2, clearing the 2.25.1 shared-memory bug that breaks all collectives on Blackwell sm_120 + PCIe. (vLLM sidesteps the same bug via its custom all-reduce.)

MoE-backend search on sm_120 — 3 fail, flashinfer_cutlass is the one that works new sm_120 datum

Bringing SGLang up on RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell required cycling MoE runner backends — a hardware-specific result not previously recorded for sm_120:
  • triton — SMEM overflow (required 147,456 B > 101,376 B hardware limit on sm_120). The fused-MoE Triton kernel assumes Hopper/larger-SMEM.
  • triton_kernel — weight-shape mismatch at _load_w2 (1,344 vs 1,024).
  • flashinfer — invalid choice (not a registered backend name).
  • flashinfer_cutlass — WORKS. Weights load (modelopt_fp8), Mamba + KV caches allocate, CUDA-graph capture completes, server serves coherent grounded answers.
Launch: --tp 2 --fp8-gemm-backend triton --moe-runner-backend flashinfer_cutlass --mamba-scheduler-strategy no_buffer --reasoning-parser nemotron_3 --kv-cache-dtype fp8_e4m3 --mem-fraction-static 0.90 --context-length 16384, env SGLANG_DISABLE_DEEP_GEMM=1.

Why g7e is latency-bound — structural, not a misconfiguration

  • One replica, not four. FP8 weights (~124 GB) exceed a single 96 GB GPU, so g7e.12xlarge (2 GPUs) runs exactly one TP=2 replica. B200/H200 (8 GPUs) pack 4 replicas (tp2×4) or 8 (tp1×8) and load-balance 130 concurrent across them (~16–32/replica). On g7e all 130 hit one replica → prefill queue explodes (TTFT 35 s at c130).
  • GDDR7 ≪ HBM3e. Prefill is bandwidth-bound; per-GPU prefill throughput is ~⅓ of B200/H200 (see metric ①). This is the dominant effect.
  • PCIe, not NVSwitch. The TP=2 all-reduce traverses PCIe instead of NVLink — adds to TPOT under load and means scaling by wider TP (TP4/TP8) would make things worse, not better. The only sane g7e scale-out is more 2-GPU replicas behind a load balancer.

Is the B200 knee compute-bound or bandwidth-bound? — and would a B300 help?

Measured GPU utilization at the c192 knee settles it: neither DCGM datum

We pinned a privileged DCGM exporter to the B200 node and sampled the profiling metrics across all 8 GPUs during a sustained c192 run (1,536 requests). Busy-window means (GR_ENGINE > 5%), even across all 8 GPUs:

DCGM metricwhat it measuresbusy meanpeak
GR_ENGINE_ACTIVEoverall SM occupancy50%82%
DRAM_ACTIVEHBM bandwidth utilization15%24%
PIPE_TENSOR_ACTIVEtensor-core (FLOP) utilization11%18%
POWER_USAGEboard power (of ~1,000 W TDP)701 W884 W

At the SLO knee the B200 is neither bandwidth-bound (HBM ~15%) nor compute-bound (tensor cores ~11%). SMs are ~50% occupied while the two units a bigger GPU would improve — HBM bandwidth and FP4/FP8 FLOPs — sit almost idle. The bottleneck is launch / scheduling overhead in the hybrid Mamba-2 + grouped-MoE decode path: at A12B-active with ~16–32 streams per replica, each step fires many small expert GEMMs that occupy SMs with dispatch and memory-movement work rather than dense tensor math (the ~50%-SM / ~11%-tensor gap is the signature). KV memory is a non-issue — 166 of 192 GB resident is almost all weights; KV headroom is ~113×.

B300 verdict: stay on B200. A p6-b300 over p6-b200 brings exactly two deltas — more HBM capacity (~288 vs ~192 GB/GPU) and more FP4 FLOPs; crucially HBM bandwidth is identical (~8 TB/s). This workload uses neither: capacity is 113× over-provisioned already, FLOPs are 89% idle, and bandwidth (the one thing B300 does not add) is the only HBM axis under any pressure. So a B300 would move none of the metrics that gate this workload — it is a no-op at materially higher cost. The headroom that is reachable lives in software, not silicon — but the specific MoE-tile-tuning lever we expected does not apply here (measured; see below). B300 verdict holds.

MoE grouped-GEMM tile tuning — measured a no-op on this build. The launch-bound signature pointed at vLLM's Triton fused-MoE tile config (per-(E,N,device,dtype) JSON; a missing entry falls back to a heuristic). We built a ray-free single-GPU tuner, generated a tuned E=512,N=1344,B200,fp8_w8a8 config, mounted it via VLLM_TUNED_CONFIG_FOLDER, and re-ran c192. Result: no change (e2e p50 6.56 s / p90 10.6 s vs baseline 6.05 / 9.05 s — within single-run cold-start noise; identical throughput). Root cause: on B200 vLLM auto-selects the FLASHINFER_TRTLLM FP8 MoE backend (logged fp8.py:396 on all 4 replicas), whose TrtLlmFp8Experts kernels are vendor-autotuned by TensorRT-LLM and never read the Triton tile JSON — only the TRITON backend's TritonExperts does. The serving logs never emit the "Using default MoE config — sub-optimal" Triton warning (the one runtime "default … sub-optimal" warning is for Mamba SSU, a different kernel). So the heuristic-fallback we tuned against was never on the production decode path. To move this lever one would have to force --moe-runner-backend triton and beat TRTLLM's own tuned GEMM — unlikely to be a net win, and untested here. Net: the launch-bound knee is real, but tile tuning is not the unlock; the practical levers remain layout (tp1×8 on H200) and a future vLLM that fixes prefix caching / Mamba-2 spec-decode.

Recommendation to customer

  1. Serve on H200 vLLM tp1×8 for the best $/token within SLO — now measured, ~½ of B200. The confirming c64/c96/c120 sweep (2026-06-12, live endpoint) shows H200 with 8 independent TP=1 replicas passes the full contractual e2e gate through c120 (measured e2e p50 5.2 s / p90 7.9 s, 0 errors) at $0.056/1M reserved (~½ of B200's cheapest SLO-passing point, $0.115/1M at its c192 knee), with the p90 crossover at ~c128 — it holds the SLO almost to the 130 target. Use the tp1×8 layout, not tp2×4: 124 GB FP8 weights fit in a single 141 GB H200, so 8 zero-collective replicas beat 4 TP=2 replicas by ~2.6× throughput here. This is the recommended deployment for this workload unless you need guaranteed margin at exactly 130 (see #2).
  2. Use B200 when the requirement is firmly 130 concurrent with margin — and know it has the most headroom above the spec. B200 vLLM tp2×4 (8-GPU NVSwitch, 4 replicas) holds the full 130 target at $0.126/1M reserved (e2e p50 4.2 s / p90 7.6 s — more tail headroom than H200's 7.9 s at c120), and keeps passing the e2e SLO well beyond it: measured to c192 at $0.115/1M (p50 6.0 s / p90 9.1 s), with the p90 crossover at ~c201 — i.e. ~55% more concurrency above the spec at an even lower per-token cost. So at the c130 spec point B200 is ~2.3× H200's rate (choose it for the NVSwitch tail margin and proven full-130 headroom); run it at the c192 knee to minimize $/token ($0.115/1M). The right B200 layout is tp2×4, not tp1×8: we swept the collective-free tp1×8 layout (the analogue of the H200 winner) at c120/c130, and it fails the e2e gate (p50 ~11.5 s, TPOT p50 ~135 ms) at ~half the throughput — unlike H200, B200's 183 GB per-GPU lets the weights fit at TP=1, but a lone Blackwell can't pace decode for ~16 concurrent streams fast enough, so tp2×4 wins decisively.
  3. g7e is a low-concurrency / residency option, not a single-node choice for this load. Unlike H200, g7e has no hidden mid-range headroom: its p90 crossover is at ~c12–14, so the single 2-GPU replica passes the SLO only across c8–~c12 (~$0.13–0.16/1M) before saturating to 7–10× over the gate. Its lower sticker $/1M ($0.105) is at c256 where e2e p90 is 144 s — unusable. For a lightly-loaded / single-user chatbot, a single g7e box is genuinely viable and responsive (meets the TTFT SLO at c8). To serve 130 concurrent within SLO you'd run ~16 TP=2 replicas (≈4× g7e.48xlarge, 32 GPUs) at c8 each (~$0.16/1M) — competitive on price but 32 GPUs to match 8 B200s, so operational complexity, not cost, decides against it.
  4. If g7e is mandated (data residency, on-prem Blackwell, capacity), use SGLang with --moe-runner-backend flashinfer_cutlass on a cu130 image — faster than vLLM here and the only working MoE backend on sm_120 — and scale by adding 2-GPU replicas, never by widening TP (PCIe all-reduce makes wider TP worse).
  5. Two levers were structurally unavailable on this model, on every platform — don't budget for them. Mamba-2 automatic prefix caching is non-functional at every TP — including the recommended TP=1 (0 hits / 4.2M queries measured on the H200 tp1×8 winner), so the RAG shared-header reuse never lowers prefill cost — the numbers above already reflect that. Speculative decoding is not deployable for Nemotron-3-Super on vLLM 0.18/0.22 at TP=2 (both MTP and n-gram crash the Mamba-2 CUDA-graph path). The throughput shown is what raw, well-batched prefill delivers; the win came instead from chunked-prefill batching tuning (see above), not from caching or speculation. Both are worth re-testing on a future vLLM that fixes the Mamba-2 graph-capture path — see the projection below for how much prefix caching could buy when it lands.

How much would prefix caching buy when it works? projection — not measured

Prefix caching is non-functional today at every TP (above; 0 hits even at TP=1), so this is a model, not a result — but it bounds the upside of re-testing on a fixed vLLM. Two facts cap the benefit: caching only removes prefill work, and only the shared header is realistically cacheable.

  • Prefill is a small slice of e2e here. Outputs are short (~75 tokens), so decode dominates: prefill (TTFT) is only ~8% of the e2e p50 at c130 and ~19% at c8. It rises to ~40% of the e2e p90 — the tail is where prefill (and queueing) actually bites.
  • Only ~⅓ of the input is cacheable. The shared system/RAG header is ~3,050 of the ~9,100 input tokens (~33%); the rest is query-specific retrieved context, and the corpus-reuse ceiling caps repeat content at ~30%. A hit removes ~33% of prefill compute (realistic), up to ~53% only with a hot corpus.

Removing 33% of prefill from the B200 tp2×4 winner:

The p90 figure is a lower bound: that tail is ~7× the p50 TTFT, i.e. mostly queueing, not compute. Cutting per-request prefill ~33% raises prefill throughput ~1.5×, relieving the queue — so the realized p90 drop could plausibly reach ~15–20% (queue-model estimate, not measured). Bottom line: single-digit-% on the median, ~10–20% on the SLO-binding tail. Applied to H200 tp1×8, a ~10–20% p90 cut at c130 (now 10.0 s) would plausibly pull it under the 9,500 ms gate — i.e. prefix caching is exactly the lever that could close H200's last ~2 concurrent (c128→130) and make the full target pass on the cheaper platform. It similarly buys headroom on B200 (higher concurrency or the longer ~243-token production output). It does not rescue g7e (c130 prefill alone is 35 s, ~4× over even after a 33% cut). It does not change the ranking (H200 cheapest within SLO, B200 for full-130 margin today), but it is the highest-value item to re-test on a future vLLM that fixes the Mamba-2 graph-capture path. Note the longer production output (~243 vs ~75 tokens here) would make e2e more decode-weighted, shrinking caching's relative e2e benefit.
