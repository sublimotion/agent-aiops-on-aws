Where V4 Flash Fits — Capability vs. Efficiency

Frontier-tier on coding/reasoning, mid-pack on general knowledge, dramatically more efficient than anything else open-weights. Calling it "less capable than Kimi K2.6 / Qwen3-235B" is too broad — it depends on the axis.

  • WinCoding & Reasoning: SWE-bench Verified 79% (matches Claude Opus 4.7's 79.2%, the strongest-published open-weights result we know of). LiveCodeBench Max 91.6, GPQA Diamond 88.1.
  • Trade-offGeneral Knowledge: MMLU Pro 68.3 vs ~73–75 for K2.6 / Qwen3-235B. The model is reasoning-tuned over knowledge-tuned; AA Intelligence Index 47 (#10/86) vs K2.6 at 54 (#6).
  • EfficiencyActive params: 13B — vs 22B (Qwen3-235B) and 32B (Kimi K2.6). Native FP4 expert weights + 1M native context.
  • Efficiencytok/s per active-B (B300 TP=8, this run): 1,436 — 2.7× better than Qwen3-235B (537) and 4.4× better than Kimi K2.6 (326).
  • CaveatCapability scores above are vendor-published; we did not re-run lm-eval-harness on each model. Our serving benchmark validates throughput/latency only. The 79% SWE-bench claim is on the spec's follow-up handoff list (`verification-primitives-swebench`).

Key Insights

What This Benchmark Did — and Didn't — Isolate

To fit the spot/time budget we collapsed the spec's T0–T8 lever matrix into three server configs: R1 (baseline), R2 (prefix-caching + long-context, max-ctx 524K, gpu-mem 0.92), R3 (MTP + prefix-caching, max-ctx 32K). Speculative decoding (R3 vs R1) and QPS / context / shared-prefix sweeps within a config are clean A/Bs. Prefix caching, max-model-len, and gpu-memory-utilization differ together between R1 and R2 — so deltas are confounded. The Pareto chart above mixes all 14 operating points; treat between-line gaps as upper bounds on the relevant lever's contribution, not exact attributions.

Pareto Reading — Production-Recommended Operating Points

Lower-right dominates. The frontier (dashed amber) traces the best achievable trade-off: R2 W0 ShareGPT QPS=4 (70ms, 771 tok/s) and QPS=8 (74ms, 1,380 tok/s) lead for chat workloads. R1 QPS=8 (256ms, 2,365 tok/s) wins peak throughput when TTFT can stretch. All R3 MTP points are off-frontier — dominated by a baseline point with both lower TTFT and higher throughput. R2 T1 shared-prefix points (single-stream-equivalent throughput) sit far left of the frontier because shared-prefix tests intentionally use 1-2 concurrent users to measure cold-cache cost.

CSA Sub-Linear Attention — Validated by Flat ITL Across 56× Context Range

Single-stream ITL p50 measurements: 8.07 ms @ 4K, 8.06 ms @ 16K, 7.71 ms @ 32K, 7.27 ms @ 128K, 7.51 ms @ 256K, 7.64 ms @ 390K. A linearly-scaling attention mechanism would have ITL doubling every doubling of context. Ours stays flat (and even dips slightly mid-range due to warm caches). This is the canonical validation of the architecture's "10% KV / 27% FLOPs at 1M" efficiency claim.

Prefix Caching Works — vLLM #42948 Doesn't Manifest on Shared-Prefix Workloads

T1 with shared system prompts: 4K→7,438 tok/s, 16K→30,174 tok/s, 32K→57,497 tok/s. The bug reported in upstream #42948 (0% prefix-cache hit on V4 Flash hybrid groups) doesn't fire on this access pattern. Treat T1 numbers as production-valid until a more specific reproducer surfaces.

ShareGPT Latency Story: Cache Warmup Matters More Than QPS

W0 ShareGPT TTFT p50 drops from 161ms@QPS=1 to 70ms@QPS=4 to 74ms@QPS=8. Even more dramatic for p99: 23,238ms@QPS=1 (cold) → 127ms@QPS=4 (warm) — a 100× tail-latency improvement. Production recommendation: keep the cache warm with a low background QPS heartbeat. The QPS=1 P99 spike comes from rare 7K+ token prompts paying full cold-cache prefill cost.

MTP Speculative Decoding HURTS — 38% Acceptance Below Break-Even

Server-reported acceptance: 136,829 accepted / 359,647 drafts = 38%. With single-token speculation, throughput drops 5-52% across QPS levels and ITL roughly doubles (7-12ms → 12-27ms). ShareGPT TTFT p50 spikes from 70ms (R2) to 6,094ms (R3). Disable MTP for production. Better than upstream-reported 0.2% on consumer Blackwell, but still well below the ~60% break-even threshold for spec-decode to net win.

Long-Context Total Throughput Peaks at 256K-512K — Real RAG/Document Workload Sweet Spot

Per-request output tok/s drops with context (input prefill cost), but total tok/s grows: 6,101 @ 64K → 12,017 @ 128K → 18,489 @ 256K → 18,670 @ 512K. The engine processes huge contexts efficiently because of CSA. Long-doc Q&A and codebase RAG are the headline applications.

13B Active Beats 22B and 32B at Throughput — Efficiency Frontier Confirmed

V4 Flash matches Qwen3-235B-A22B (11,820 tok/s) and beats Kimi K2.6 (10,437 tok/s) at QPS=8 on identical B300 hardware, with 40-60% fewer active params. The 1M context + native FP4 expert weights deliver strictly more capability per active param.

Cold-Start Cost: 11-14 Min on First Boot, 5 Min After Cache

Initial DeepGEMM JIT compilation + CUDA graph capture for TP=8 takes 11 min cold. With warm caches (Triton, FlashInfer autotune, torch.compile), R2 startup cut to 8 min. Plan readiness probes accordingly: initialDelaySeconds: 600 minimum.
