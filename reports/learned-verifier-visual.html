2. Calibration (ECE) — The RL Gate

ECE measures whether predicted probabilities match reality. Two verifiers with identical AUC but 7x ECE difference: one works for RL, the other collapses training.

What ECE Means

When the model says "80% confident", do 80% of those patches actually pass?

ECE RangeAssessmentUse Case
< 0.05ExcellentSafe for RL reward
0.05 – 0.10GoodRL with monitoring
0.10 – 0.30MarginalRanking only, recalibrate first
> 0.30PoorNot usable as continuous signal
Weather analogy: When the forecast says "80% rain," it should rain ~80% of those days. ECE = 0.30 means the forecast is off by 30 percentage points on average. You can still tell rainy days from dry (good AUC), but the numbers are wrong — and RL multiplies by those numbers.

3. The Feature Convergence Discovery

Pivot analysis and tiny-judge independently identified the same behavioral signals as the strongest predictors.

The surprise: Phase 3 forward selection chose zero LLM-based features. All 4 selected features are behavioral: total_cost_usd, action_pct_edit, adversarial_review_used, loop_count. Efficient agents succeed; thrashing agents fail. This is the quantitative signature of Parkinson's Law for agents.

4. Signal Contribution — Does Combining Help?

Adding LLM signals (v009, debate, SVG) to behavioral features has different effects on discrimination vs calibration.

Two profiles emerge:
Profile A (max discrimination): 4-feature RF, AUC=0.727, P@R30=0.933, ECE=0.072, cost=$0
Profile B (max calibration): 60-feature RF + Platt, AUC=0.707, ECE=0.026, cost=$0.054/patch — optimal RL reward
