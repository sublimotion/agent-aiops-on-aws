Pair-length scaling · c=4 · k=50

pairs/s scales inverse-linearly with pair_length. Halving the pair doubles pairs/s, meaning per-pair latency is proportional to sequence length — L40S is compute-bound on attention across the whole pair, not request-overhead-bound. L=512 is the capacity headline at 700 pairs/s.

pairs/s per pair_length (L=4096 → config FAIL)

L=4096 failed 50/50 with HTTP 400. The chat template adds ~1 token of overhead; requested 4097 tokens > max_model_len=4096. Fix: deploy with max_model_len=4352, or cap sweep at 3840. Captured intentionally as a data point, not a capacity failure.

Methodology callout · the c=256 "surprise"

c=256 tied c=4 on pairs/s and had lower p99 than c=64. That's not vLLM magic — it's a burst-mode artifact. The aiohttp semaphore admits up to c=256 in flight, but n_steady=50 means only 50 requests are ever dispatched per level. Real in-flight peaks at ~50, which is below vLLM's max-num-seqs=64 cap. So c=16/64/256 all collapse to the same batch, and the only difference is how quickly the burst is admitted. Larger bursts let vLLM form more uniform continuous-batching groups on the first steps, giving tighter p99 at c=256 (6,355 ms) than at c=64 (6,772 ms) — a 6% effect, inside noise but consistent with the scheduler theory.
Warning. Burst-mode sweeps misrepresent saturation. To measure true queue behavior, drive with a sustained arrival rate above service rate, not a fixed-batch burst bounded by n_steady. The c=4 knee here is real for this burst shape; the "c=256 is fine" signal is an artifact.
