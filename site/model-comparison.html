Key Findings

MoE Batching Is the Throughput Multiplier

All MoE models show dramatic throughput scaling with concurrency: GLM-5 goes 50→2,602 tok/s (52x), Nemotron 77→1,449 tok/s (19x), Qwen3-Next 146→3,389 tok/s (23x). Only a fraction of parameters are active per token, so batching amortizes routing overhead. Dense Devstral only scales ~3x. Design for concurrent load, not single-stream.

Hybrid Architectures Break the Toolchain

Every hybrid model (Mamba+Attention) hit framework incompatibilities: Qwen3-Next's HMA blocks all KV offloading in vLLM; GLM-5's NSA breaks LMCache; Nemotron's Mamba-2 state crashes NIXL disaggregated transfer. The only successful KV offloading for hybrids was SGLang HiCache (GLM-5, 2.86x). Framework support for hybrid architectures lags 3-6 months behind dense transformer support.

Prefix Caching Dominates All Other Optimizations

Across all models and hardware, prefix caching delivered the largest single improvement: +77% throughput for Qwen3-Next at 1000 concurrent, -46% TTFT for Kimi K2.5 multi-turn, 97% hit rate with shared prefixes. KV offloading (LMCache, Dynamo) provided marginal gains (1.07-1.31x TTFT) and introduced overhead under load. On H200-class hardware, HBM is large enough that KV pressure rarely develops — prefix caching is the critical optimization, not offloading.

Devstral: Best Quality Per GPU

At just 1 GPU per replica, Devstral matches Qwen3-Next on BFCL (91.7%) and coding eval (100%) while being 5x faster per request (955ms vs 4,830ms). 97% SVG recall on functional eval versus 0% for Qwen3-Next. Zero failures in swarm testing. For coding agents where quality and reliability matter more than raw throughput, Devstral on a single RTX PRO 6000 is the most cost-efficient option.

Head-of-Line Blocking Is Universal — Disaggregation Is the Fix

Every aggregated deployment (vLLM and SGLang) shows TTFT p90 explosion at high concurrency: Nemotron 337ms→44.5s, Qwen3-Next 238ms→64s. Prefill and decode share GPUs, causing mutual interference. Disaggregated prefill/decode is the architectural solution, but it's blocked for all hybrid models (Mamba state transfer unsupported). Only dense transformer models can use disagg mode today.

B200 vs H200 vs RTX PRO 6000: Different Strengths

B200 (p6, NVLink): Best for very large models (GLM-5 744B, Nemotron-Super). 183 GB HBM per GPU, fast NVLink interconnect. ~$18/hr spot.
H200 (p5en, NVSwitch): Best for throughput-intensive production workloads. Mature ecosystem, all frameworks work. 141 GB HBM. ~$60-98/hr.
RTX PRO 6000 (g7e, PCIe): Best cost per token. Per-GPU throughput matches H200 at ~4x lower cost. 96 GB GDDR7. But PCIe limits multi-GPU training (NCCL bug), MTP hurts, and DP+EP underperforms TP. ~$17/hr.
